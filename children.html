Children

Supporting healthy breathing, sleep and facial development.

Children's airway care is about more than a symptom — it's about how breathing, sleep and growth shape each other. Early, specialist-led evaluation helps parents understand what's happening and what to do next.

Signs to watch

When parents often reach out.

These patterns are not a diagnosis — but they are reasons to consider a specialist evaluation.

Mouth breathing during the day or during sleep
Loud or persistent snoring
Restless sleep or frequent waking
Daytime tiredness or difficulty waking
Poor concentration or attention at school
Dental crowding or a narrow upper jaw
Picky eating, chewing difficulties or slow feeding
Recurrent mouth breathing with nasal congestion

This is not a diagnosis. Please consult a qualified specialist for advice specific to your child.

Facial development & airway

How breathing shapes the growing face.

In children, the way they breathe influences how the face and jaw develop — and vice versa. Nasal breathing supports forward growth of the upper jaw; habitual mouth breathing can shift that pattern over time.

This is why we look at breathing, oral function and facial growth together, rather than treating each in isolation. Small, timely interventions during growth years can make a meaningful difference — which is why early evaluation matters.

Every child is different. Assessment findings and any recommendations are specific to your child's situation.

Through the growing years

What to watch, stage by stage.

The relationship between airway, breathing and growth looks different at each age. Here is what often brings families to us.

Early childhood

Feeding, nasal breathing, sleep quality, mouth breathing during rest.

School age

Snoring, restless sleep, daytime attention, dental crowding, oral habits.

Pre-teen

Facial growth, jaw development, orthodontic planning, persistent snoring.

Teens

Daytime fatigue, breathing during sport, sleep concerns, jaw discomfort.

This is general guidance only, not a diagnostic tool. Please consult a specialist for advice specific to your child.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

Is snoring normal in children?

Occasional snoring can be normal, but persistent snoring in children may deserve specialist evaluation. Please consult a qualified clinician for advice specific to your child.

When should I be concerned about my child's breathing?

Persistent mouth breathing, loud snoring, restless sleep or daytime tiredness are worth discussing with a specialist. Early evaluation helps clarify what, if anything, needs attention.

What happens during a children's consultation?

Consultations are gentle and age-appropriate. We listen to your concerns, assess airway, breathing, sleep and oral function, and explain what we find in plain language.

Do you work with other specialists?

Yes. Where needed, we coordinate with ENT, paediatric, dental, orthodontic and myofunctional colleagues as part of a whole-picture approach.

At what age can a child be assessed?

Children of all ages can be assessed. The approach is adapted to your child's age and comfort.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly. If you have previous reports, please bring them to the consultation.

Will treatment be recommended at the first visit?

Not always. The first visit is about understanding. Recommendations depend on what we find and are explained before any decision is made.
