Adults

Specialist evaluation for breathing, snoring and sleep.

Breathing changes over a lifetime — and so do the concerns that come with it. Whether it's snoring, waking unrefreshed, or a lingering feeling that something is off, a specialist evaluation helps clarify what's happening and what to do next.

Common concerns

Why adults come to see us.

These are the patterns we most often assess. If any of them feel familiar, a consultation can help bring clarity.

Snoring

Habitual, loud or disruptive snoring — for you or your partner.

Waking unrefreshed

Sleeping enough hours, but still tired in the morning.

Daytime fatigue

Difficulty staying alert through the day, or after meals.

Mouth breathing

Habitual mouth breathing during the day or during sleep.

Nasal blockage

Persistent stuffiness or difficulty breathing through the nose.

Jaw & TMJ discomfort

Jaw pain, clicking, clenching or morning jaw tightness.

Sleep apnea concerns

Pauses in breathing during sleep, reported by a partner.

Morning headaches

Waking with a dry mouth, headache or feeling unrested.

What we assess

A structured, adult-focused evaluation.

Breathing during sleep, nasal function, jaw mechanics and lifestyle all interact. We look at them together — not in isolation.

Airway & nasal patency

How air flows through the nose and upper airway at rest and during sleep.

Sleep quality & patterns

Sleep timing, restfulness, snoring and reported pauses in breathing.

Breathing during sleep

Patterns that may suggest obstructive or sleep-related breathing concerns.

Jaw & TMJ function

Jaw movement, clenching, clicking and how this relates to breathing.

Oral & tongue posture

Resting tongue position, oral habits, and effects on the airway.

Lifestyle & risk factors

Weight, activity, alcohol, smoking, medications and stress — where relevant.

Sleep & breathing

Why breathing during sleep matters.

Sleep is when the body recovers. When breathing is disrupted — whether by nasal obstruction, jaw position or airway narrowing — recovery is interrupted, often without the person noticing.

This is why daytime fatigue, morning headaches and difficulty concentrating can sometimes trace back to what happens at night. A specialist evaluation helps connect these dots.

This is general information only and is not a diagnosis. Please consult a qualified clinician for advice specific to you.

FAQ

Questions adults ask

Is snoring something to worry about?

Snoring can be harmless, but persistent or loud snoring — especially with daytime tiredness — may deserve a specialist evaluation. Please consult a qualified clinician for advice specific to you.

When should I see a specialist for sleep-related concerns?

If you wake unrefreshed, feel tired during the day, snore heavily, or have been told you stop breathing during sleep, a specialist evaluation can help clarify what is happening.

Do you diagnose sleep apnea?

We assess airway and breathing patterns and, where appropriate, coordinate with sleep medicine colleagues for formal sleep studies and diagnosis.

Can adults develop airway problems later in life?

Yes. Weight changes, nasal obstruction, jaw changes and ageing can all affect breathing during sleep. Evaluation helps identify contributing factors.

Do you treat TMJ or jaw discomfort?

We assess jaw function as part of an airway-focused evaluation and coordinate with dental, orthodontic or oral medicine colleagues when needed.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly. If you have previous reports or sleep studies, please bring them to the consultation.

Will I get a diagnosis on the first visit?

Not always. The first visit is about understanding. If further testing or coordination is needed, we explain that clearly.
